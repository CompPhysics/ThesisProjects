The reorganization is promising and, in my view, scientifically
stronger than a proposal organized primarily around platforms or
methodologies. It gives the project a clear intellectual spine: first
establish the theory of private estimation, then develop
structure-aware learning methods, and finally validate them
experimentally and computationally. This is a coherent progression
from foundations to algorithms to demonstrations.

The main risk is that the current objectives are still too broad and
that WP3 is partly a validation plan rather than a research
objective. I would recommend making the central scientific question
more explicit:

> How can one infer and learn physically meaningful properties of energy-constrained bosonic quantum systems from finite, noisy, and privacy-constrained data, while exploiting known Hamiltonian structure?

That question gives the three work packages a sharper relationship.

### WP1

WP1 is the strongest and most conceptually distinctive component. The
combination of differential privacy, bosonic continuous variables, and
energy constraints is potentially novel. It should, however, specify
the privacy model very carefully. In particular, the proposal should
distinguish privacy for:

- individual measurement records;
- experimental runs or trajectories;
- quantum states or channels;
- and training datasets containing information about multiple systems.

You should also state which privacy notion will be used—standard
\((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-differential privacy, Rényi differential
privacy, or a related formulation—and how privacy composes across
repeated measurements.

The energy constraint should not be treated merely as a technical
assumption. It is likely to be central to the theory: it can control
otherwise unbounded observables, define admissible state classes, and
determine finite-sample utility bounds. A particularly strong WP1
would establish minimax rates or confidence bounds for estimating
first and second moments, covariance matrices, and selected
Hamiltonian parameters under both energy and privacy constraints.

The extension from Gaussian to “near-Gaussian” states needs a precise
definition. Possible choices include bounded higher cumulants,
perturbative non-Gaussianity, low-rank corrections in a Gaussian
basis, or a controlled distance from a Gaussian reference
state. Without such a definition, the scope is too open-ended.

### WP2

WP2 provides the necessary bridge from estimation theory to
learning. “Hamiltonian-guided structured learning” is a good
direction, but the proposal should say exactly what is learned:
Hamiltonian coefficients, states, Wigner functions, observables,
dynamical maps, or predictive quantities.

The Wigner-function task is scientifically interesting but potentially
ill-posed. Wigner functions can be highly sensitive to finite-sample
noise and may take negative values, so the proposal should emphasize
physical consistency and uncertainty quantification rather than only
pointwise reconstruction. It may be preferable to formulate the
problem as learning a state or a characteristic function subject to
positivity, normalization, and energy constraints, with the Wigner
function recovered as a derived representation.

A strong version of WP2 would develop estimators that combine:

- Hamiltonian sparsity or locality;
- symmetries and conservation laws;
- Gaussian reference models;
- positivity and normalization constraints;
- bounded-energy regularization;
- and privacy-preserving data processing.

The transition to non-Gaussian systems should be incremental rather
than a second full research programme. For example, begin with weakly
non-Gaussian corrections, low-order polynomial Hamiltonians, or states
generated by a restricted gate family.

### WP3

WP3 may be reframed. At present it combines GBS, quantum-inspired
EM, Magne, and non-Gaussian transfer, which makes it look like several
independent projects. The platform-based tasks should instead be
presented as validation environments for specific theoretical claims
from WP1 and WP2.

For example:

- GBS can validate private and structure-aware inference for Gaussian or photon-number distributions, but it does not automatically validate generic bosonic state learning.
- Quantum-inspired EM is only useful if it solves a clearly defined latent-variable inference problem and has a demonstrable connection to the models developed in WP2.
- Magne should be included only if it can test a meaningful part of the proposed inference pipeline. Otherwise it risks looking like an aspirational platform insertion rather than a scientifically necessary component.
- Transfer to non-Gaussian learning should be a final stress test, not an additional objective of equal status.

I would organize WP3 around benchmark questions: Does exploiting
Hamiltonian structure improve sample complexity? What privacy–accuracy
trade-off is observed experimentally? Do theoretical confidence bounds
remain informative under realistic noise? Can the algorithms transfer
from simulated Gaussian data to programmable sampling data?

### Suggested revised structure

A sharper formulation might be:

**WP1 — Foundations:**  

Develop privacy-preserving, energy-constrained estimation theory for
Gaussian and controlled near-Gaussian bosonic systems.

**WP2 — Structure-aware inference:**  

Develop physically constrained learning methods that incorporate
Hamiltonian structure, symmetries, and Gaussian reference models, with
finite-sample guarantees and privacy–utility bounds.

**WP3 — Experimental and scalable validation:**  

Test the theory and algorithms on programmable Gaussian Boson
Sampling, quantum simulators, and—where scientifically
justified—larger quantum-computing platforms.

Each work package should have one or two explicit deliverables, such as:

- a minimax or non-asymptotic estimation theorem;
- a private estimator with a proved privacy–accuracy trade-off;
- a structure-aware learning algorithm with physical-consistency guarantees;
- and a benchmark demonstrating improvement over unstructured and non-private baselines.

Overall, I think the proposed direction is well aligned with a broad
scientific vision centered on quantum machine learning, quantum
information, and scalable inference. The proposal will be strongest if
statistical inference is clearly the scientific contribution and the
hardware platforms are presented as progressively more demanding tests
of that contribution. I would narrow the near-Gaussian/non-Gaussian
scope, make privacy and energy models mathematically explicit, and
convert WP3 from a collection of platforms into a hypothesis-driven
validation programme.
